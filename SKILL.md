# Competitive Ads Extractor

Research publicly accessible advertising examples and turn observations into evidence-based competitive insights. Use research to inform original campaigns, not to copy competitors.

## When to use

- Researching competitor advertising and positioning.
- Comparing messaging across platforms or brands.
- Identifying recurring customer problems, value propositions, and calls to action.
- Building a reference set for campaign planning.
- Tracking public creative themes over time.

## Research workflow

### 1. Define scope

Establish competitors, platforms, geography, language, time period, audience context, questions, and output format.

### 2. Collect public evidence

Use platform-provided ad libraries, public campaign pages, or authorized data providers. Follow platform terms, rate limits, and access controls.

Capture available metadata such as advertiser, platform, date, headline, body copy, format, landing page, and publicly disclosed targeting details. Record collection date and source links.

Do not bypass authentication, access restrictions, CAPTCHAs, or technical controls. Do not harvest private personal data or infer sensitive personal characteristics.

### 3. Organize and classify

Group ads by customer problem, value proposition, product positioning, publicly disclosed audience, creative format, offer, proof point, call to action, and campaign timing. Separate direct observations from interpretation.

### 4. Analyze patterns

Identify recurring themes, differences, changes over time, and evidence gaps. Do not claim an ad performs well unless reliable performance data exists. Frequency or repetition alone does not prove effectiveness.

### 5. Recommend original experiments

Provide testable messaging hypotheses and differentiated creative ideas. Do not reproduce competitor copy, images, layouts, or distinctive campaign concepts in infringing or misleading ways.

## Suggested outputs

- Research summary with collection date and source links.
- Competitor messaging matrix.
- Theme and creative-format breakdown.
- Examples with evidence-based annotations.
- Original campaign hypotheses and A/B test ideas.
- CSV or spreadsheet of public ad metadata, where useful.

## Legal and ethical boundaries

Use public and authorized sources. Respect platform terms, copyright, trademarks, privacy laws, and data retention limits. Avoid impersonation, unauthorized scraping, and attempts to obtain non-public campaign data.

## Quality standard

Cite evidence for factual claims. Label estimates and hypotheses. State limitations such as incomplete archives and unavailable performance metrics.
